Abdulkarim G. Mohammed is a software engineer in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia. By day he builds AI platforms as a senior full-stack engineer and runs Eaglix Tech, an agency connecting Ethiopian engineers with clients worldwide. He studied software engineering at Addis Ababa University.

He is a student of knowledge, not a scholar. Sīmā is where he sets down, in order, what he is learning about why Islam is true, with the sources that taught him, so you can check them yourself. Away from the keyboard, it's chess and football, usually from midfield.

## What this site is for

Sīmā makes one case, step by step: that God exists, that Islam is His religion, and that the doubts raised against it have answers. It is arranged as a course in four parts, best read in [order](/start):

1. **Foundations.** How we know anything at all, and the kinds of argument that can carry a conclusion: deduction, induction and inference to the best explanation.
2. **The existence of God.** The classical proofs, premise by premise: Ibn Sīnā's argument from contingency, al-Ghazālī's argument from origination, Ibn Taymiyya on the innate disposition (_fiṭra_) and the signs, and the argument from design.
3. **The truth of Islam.** The evidence for the prophethood of Muḥammad ﷺ and for the Qur'an, and why Islam alone holds revelation whole, while other religions keep some of it mixed with error.
4. **Answering doubts.** The _shubuhāt_ raised by atheism, liberalism, feminism and other worldviews, and particular arguments such as the problem of evil.

It is written by a Muslim, openly and from conviction. That makes fairness more important, not less: every opposing view is stated at its strongest, in its own words where possible, before it is answered, and no one is mocked. It is written for two kinds of reader at once: Muslims who want to know why they believe, and people of other faiths or none who want to see the case made properly.

Nothing here is a fatwā; for a ruling on your own situation, ask a qualified scholar. And nothing here assumes you already know the terms. Arabic words are explained where they appear and collected in the [glossary](/glossary).

## The name

_Sīmā_ (سيماء) is the mark by which something is known. The Qur'an uses the word for the visible sign that identifies a people (2:273, 48:29). Truth has its marks too, and this site is an attempt to set them out clearly enough to be recognised.

## Sources and translations

Primary texts are always quoted in full, with their reference and translator, and set apart from commentary so you can tell the source from the argument made about it.

- **Qur'an.** Arabic text with an English translation, usually Sahih International unless another is named beside the verse.
- **Ḥadīth.** Quoted from the major collections with collection and number. Grades are those given by the collection or by the scholar named in the text.
- **Classical works.** Ibn Sīnā, al-Ghazālī, Ibn Taymiyya and others are cited by work and section, with the edition or translation used.
- **Bible and other scripture.** Quoted from the translation named beside each passage, most often the ESV.
- **Transliteration.** A simplified academic system, described in [a note on transliteration](/blog/sample-transliteration).

Every post links to its source file. If you find an error in a quotation, a reference or an argument, use **Suggest a correction** at the end of the post.
