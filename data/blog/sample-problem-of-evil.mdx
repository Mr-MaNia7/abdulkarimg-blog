## The argument at its strongest

The objection comes in two forms, and a fair answer has to meet both.

The **logical** form claims a contradiction: a God who is all-good would want to remove evil, a God who is all-powerful could, and yet evil exists.[^mackie] If that were a true contradiction, no amount of evidence could rescue belief in God.

The **evidential** form is more modest: it grants there may be reasons for some suffering, but says there is so much of it, so apparently pointless, that a good God's existence is unlikely.[^rowe]

<Sidenote>Most philosophers today, theist and atheist alike, hold that the logical form fails, and argue about the evidential form instead.</Sidenote>

## The hidden premise

Both forms rest on a premise they rarely state: that a good God's aim would be a world as comfortable as possible for us. The Qur'an names a different aim:

<Quran
  ref="67:2"
  arabic="الَّذِي خَلَقَ الْمَوْتَ وَالْحَيَاةَ لِيَبْلُوَكُمْ أَيُّكُمْ أَحْسَنُ عَمَلًا ۚ وَهُوَ الْعَزِيزُ الْغَفُورُ"
  translation="[He] who created death and life to test you [as to] which of you is best in deed – and He is the Exalted in Might, the Forgiving."
  translator="Sahih International"
/>

A test with nothing at stake tests nothing. Patience needs hardship, courage needs danger, and generosity needs someone in need.

## What we cannot see

The evidential form depends on suffering looking _pointless_. But looking pointless to us is evidence of pointlessness only if we would expect to see the point. If God's knowledge exceeds ours as far as the argument itself assumes, we should expect not to.

<Callout type="clarification">
This answer is about the argument. It is not a way of explaining away a particular person's grief, and should never be offered as one.
</Callout>

## Not the whole account

Finally, the argument weighs suffering against a ledger that ends at death. Islam does not. Justice delayed to the Hereafter is not justice denied, and the answer is incomplete without the parts of this course on the afterlife and on hell and divine justice.
