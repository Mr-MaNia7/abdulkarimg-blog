## The Ultimate Question of Existence

For centuries, thinkers across human history have found themselves confronted by one fundamental, inescapable question: _Why is there something rather than nothing?_

When we observe the world around us—trees, stars, oceans, subatomic particles, and human beings—we notice a shared characteristic: none of these things had to exist. Their non-existence is easily conceivable, yet they exist nonetheless. How do we account for the ultimate foundation of reality?

To resolve this existential mystery, the legendary 11th-century Islamic philosopher and polymath Ibn Sīnā (known to the West as Avicenna) formulated a landmark work of rational deduction known as the <Term translit="burhān">Burhān</Term> (The Demonstration). Rather than relying on fragile empirical assumptions or dynamic physical models that change with every scientific generation, the Burhān provides a timeless, purely logical framework. It establishes the foundational grounding of reality through a rigorous process of elimination.

> Existence cannot consist solely of dependent, temporary things; it requires a foundational, independent reality.

In contemporary discussions surrounding the origin of the cosmos, two primary competing worldviews emerge:

## The Three Building Blocks of Reality: Mapping Existence

Before evaluating why the physical cosmos exists, Ibn Sīnā constructs an intuitive modal map. He categorizes everything conceivable into three exhaustive logical categories based on how its nature relates to existence:

| Category name                           | Core definition                                                                                                                                         | Concrete/logical example                                            |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Impossible Existence (_Muḥāl_, مُحال)   | Cannot exist under any circumstances because its definition contains an internal logical contradiction.                                                 | A square circle; a married bachelor                                 |
| Contingent Existence (_Mumkin_, مُمكِن) | Can exist or not exist; its essence does not guarantee its existence, making it dependent on an external cause, prior conditions, or constituent parts. | A wooden chair, a planet, a galaxy, or the physical universe itself |
| Necessary Existence (_Wājib_, واجِب)    | Cannot not exist; completely uncaused, self-sufficient, non-composite, and independent in its essence.                                                  | The ultimate source and originator of all existence                 |

### Why Physical Observation Forces Reality into the “Contingent” Category

Before testing any causal scenarios, we must ask: Why does everything we observe in nature fall squarely into the Contingent (_Mumkin_) category?

Consider any physical object—for example, a wooden chair. The chair does not exist by virtue of its own identity; it depends on wood, screws, tools, and a carpenter. Furthermore, it is composed of spatial parts (legs, seat, backrest) and subatomic particles. Because it is composite, mutable, and depends on constituent pieces arranged in a specific state, we can easily conceive of the chair missing a part or not existing at all without generating a logical contradiction (unlike a square circle).

This principle applies universally across scale. Whether inspecting an atom, a human body, a star, or the entire physical continuum of space-time, every physical entity is composed of parts, bound by physical parameters, and susceptible to rearrangement. Because no physical object contains the reason for its own existence within its own essence, physical reality as a whole is demonstrably contingent.

With these three modal categories firmly anchored, we can now utilize the power of logical elimination to discover what holds reality together.

## The Process of Elimination: Why Dependent Things Cannot Stand Alone

If every observable aspect of our physical cosmos is contingent—meaning it depends on external factors or underlying components—how do we explain the existence of contingent reality as a whole?

Rationally, there are only four theoretical explanations. By subjecting each scenario to logical analysis, we can systematically eliminate those that fail.

### Elimination #1: Coming from Nothing (Ex Nihilo without Cause)

Can contingent reality bring itself into existence out of absolute non-existence without any cause?

- **No Effect Without a Cause:** Absolute non-existence (_ʿadam_) is the total absence of reality; it possesses no causal properties, no ontological status, and zero power to produce an effect.
- **Self-Creation is a Logical Contradiction:** For an entity to bring itself into existence, it would have to exert causal power before it existed. It would need to exist prior to its own existence, which is a self-contradictory absurdity.

As emphasized by classical scholars such as Ibn Taymiyyah and Al-Ghazālī, this elimination mirrors the self-evident dilemma articulated in the Qur'an:

Because non-existence cannot exert power and self-creation is inherently contradictory, we eliminate uncaused emergence out of pure nothingness.

### Elimination #2: Circular Dependency (Circular Regress)

Could contingent reality be explained by a closed loop of causation, where Cause A produces Cause B, Cause B produces Cause C, and Cause C produces Cause A?

Ibn Sīnā demonstrates the rational absurdity of circular causation by examining the logical relationship between priority and posteriority:

1. **Priority of Cause:** If X depends on Z for its existence, then Z must be logically prior to X (meaning Z must exist to grant existence to X).
2. **Posteriority of Effect:** If Z simultaneously depends on X for its existence, then X must be logically prior to Z, making Z posterior (subsequent) to X.
3. **The Logical Collapse:** If X depends on Z and Z depends on X, then X must be prior to Z, which is prior to X. Thus, X would have to be logically prior to itself.
4. **Conclusion:** X would have to exist before it existed in order to grant existence to Z, so that Z could grant existence to X.

When an entity’s existence depends on something that is essentially posterior to itself, existence can never occur. Therefore, circular causation cancels itself out logically.

### Elimination #3: Infinite Regress of Dependent Things (The Infinite Multiverse Fallacy)

A common objection raised by modern skeptics is that an ultimate First Cause is unnecessary if contingent causes extend backward infinitely in time or across an infinite chain of multiverse bubbles. This objection fails because it conflates temporal sequences with ontological dependency and ignores three crucial logical principles:

#### Principle 1: Ibn Sīnā’s Middle Cause Argument (Al-ʿIllah al-Mutawassiṭah)

In _Al-Shifā’_, Ibn Sīnā demonstrates that within any causal chain, elements serve specific modal functions:

- **First Cause:** A First Cause is causing but uncaused.
- **Middle Cause:** A Middle Cause (_al-ʿillah al-mutawassiṭah_) is both caused by a prior element and the cause of a subsequent element.
- **Final Effect:** A Final Effect is caused but causes nothing further.

By definition, a middle cause cannot exist independently; it functions merely as a conduit passing along causal efficacy from a prior source. In an infinite chain composed exclusively of dependent things, every single element assumes the modal status of a middle cause. However, a causal chain consisting entirely of middle causes without a First Cause is a chain composed solely of intermediate links with no anchor. Without an initiating First Cause to supply causal efficacy, the entire infinite chain of middle causes collapses into non-existence.

#### Principle 2: Duns Scotus’s Causal Distinction: Accidentally vs. Essentially Ordered Chains

To fully grasp why infinite regresses fail, medieval philosopher John Duns Scotus formulated a critical distinction often overlooked in modern debates:

**Accidentally Ordered Causes (Temporal).** A linear sequence across time where the cause can cease to exist while the effect continues (e.g., a grandfather generates a father, who generates a son; the grandfather can die, yet the son remains alive). An infinite temporal chain of accidental causes does not inherently violate logic because each element possesses independent sustaining power once generated.

**Essentially Ordered Causes (Ontological/Hierarchical).** A simultaneous structure of dependency where the primary cause must actively sustain every intermediate cause at the exact moment the effect occurs (e.g., a person moves a stick, which moves a stone). If the person stops pushing, the stick and stone instantly stop moving.

The universe's existence relies on an essentially ordered chain of ontological sustaining conditions at every moment. An infinite regress of essentially ordered dependent causes is impossible because no element possesses intrinsic existence to sustain the next.

#### Principle 3: Ibn Sīnā’s Set Theory Argument (Jumlah)

In _Al-Ishārāt_, Ibn Sīnā gathers all contingent existences into a single total set—a _Jumlah_.

- **Inherited Contingency:** If every individual member inside a set is contingent, the total set (_jumlah_) is also contingent.
- **The Infinity Fallacy:** Expanding the set to contain an infinite number of contingent items (or an infinite multiverse) does not magically transform the set into a necessary being. Summing zero self-sufficiency to zero self-sufficiency—even infinitely—never equals one self-sufficient reality.
- **The Boundary Constraint:** As shown in the visual boundary above, any factor that supplies existence to the total contingent set cannot reside inside the set (otherwise it would be causing itself). The supplier of existence must sit strictly OUTSIDE the contingent set boundary.

### Elimination #4: The Universe/Multiverse as a “Brute Fact” or Necessary Being

Could the physical universe (or an infinite multiverse) itself be the uncaused “Necessary Being”?

Skeptics sometimes attempt to bypass the need for God by claiming the universe is simply a “brute fact” that exists necessarily. This claim conflates causal contingency with mereological dependency (compositional reliance).

#### Distinction Between Temporal Causality and Mereological Dependency (Tarkīb)

As highlighted in Ibn Rushd’s (Averroes) foundational critiques of modal taxonomy, we must strictly distinguish between causal contingency (a temporal cause bringing something into being) and mereological dependency (_Tarkīb_—dependency based on composition).

Even if one posits that the physical cosmos had no temporal beginning, its composition makes it non-causally dependent. The Argument from Composition (Tarkīb) demonstrates why any composite physical structure cannot be a Necessary Being:

- **Composition Entails Dependency:** Anything composed of components depends on those constituent pieces for its structure and existence. A whole object cannot exist without its parts.
- **Ibn Taymiyyah's Definition of a “Piece”:** To prevent confusing a physical part with an essential attribute, Ibn Taymiyyah precisely defined a piece (_ju'z_) as anything that can be materially added, removed, broken off, torn off, or cut off from a substratum. This is fundamentally distinct from an essential attribute (_talāzumī_), such as a triangle's property of having three sides, which cannot be materially detached from its essence.
- **The Composite Cosmos:** The physical cosmos—and any theoretical multiverse—is composed of materially detachable pieces: subatomic particles, localized fields, changing energy states, and physical dimensions.
- **Rearrangeability:** Anything composed of attachable and detachable pieces can be abstractly or physically rearranged into different configurations. Because its current arrangement is not logically unique or necessary, its specific state is contingent.

Because the universe is composite (_murakkab_), it depends on its underlying components and their precise arrangement. A composite object cannot be self-sufficient (Wājib al-Wujūd); it stands in constant need of a unifying factor to sustain its specific composition.

Having eliminated self-creation, circular dependency, infinite regress, and cosmic brute facts, only one logical destination remains.

## The Inescapable Conclusion: The Necessary Being (Wājib al-Wujūd)

With all four alternative scenarios eliminated as logically impossible, we arrive at the positive, inescapable conclusion of the Burhān:

> Existence is anchored in a singular, uncaused, independent reality—a Necessary Being (Wājib al-Wujūd).

Based on the rigorous analytical constraints established by Ibn Sīnā, Al-Ghazālī, John Duns Scotus, and Ibn Taymiyyah, this Necessary Being possesses precise attributes:

### Outside the Contingent Set

As proven by Ibn Sīnā’s set boundary analysis (_jumlah_), the Necessary Being must exist externally to the set of all dependent things to avoid circularity and supply existence to the collection.

### Uncaused & Self-Sufficient

As Scotus and Ibn Sīnā demonstrated, it requires no prior cause, sustaining conditions, or external factors; it exists purely by virtue of its own essence (_bi-dhātih_).

### Simple & Non-Composite (Aḥad)

As Ibn Taymiyyah and Ibn Sīnā proved through the argument from Tarkīb, the Necessary Being cannot be composed of detachable physical pieces or external components. It is absolutely simple and non-composite, eliminating any possibility of physical breakdown, change, or compositional dependency.

### Volitional Preponderator (Murajjiḥ) & Volitional Particularizer (Takhṣīṣ)

As established by Al-Ghazālī’s cosmological proofs, because a contingent state has an equal possibility of existing or not existing, there must exist a Preponderator (Murajjiḥ) that exerts Particularization (Takhṣīṣ). Takhṣīṣ is the exercise of Divine Will—choosing and tipping the scales (_tarjīḥ_) to actualize one specific, concrete possible world over another without relying on an antecedent material trigger.

## Summary & Key Takeaways for the Learner

To consolidate this architectural journey through scholastic logic, let us summarize the primary logical progression of the Burhān:

### 3-Step Summary: The Logic of Contingency

1. **All Observed Reality is Contingent:** Every physical object in nature is composite, constructed of detachable pieces (Tarkīb), and subject to rearrangement. Because physical entities depend on constituent parts and conditions, they cannot account for their own existence.
2. **Chains of Dependency Require an Anchor:** An infinite regress of dependent things (or an infinite multiverse) fails to explain reality. A chain composed entirely of middle causes (Al-ʿIllah al-Mutawassiṭah) lacks an initiating source of efficacy, and expanding a set of contingent items (Jumlah) infinitely still leaves the total set dependent on an external anchor.
3. **Logic Demands an Independent First Cause:** The systematic elimination of self-creation, circular causation, infinite regress, and material brute facts leaves a single logical necessity: an uncaused, non-composite, volitional Necessary Being (Wājib al-Wujūd) that grounds all existence.

By engaging the analytical rigor of classical metaphysics, we discover that belief in an ultimate Creator is not an ungrounded leap of faith. Instead, it stands as an inescapable, logically necessary anchor that satisfies the rational mind's search for foundational truth.
