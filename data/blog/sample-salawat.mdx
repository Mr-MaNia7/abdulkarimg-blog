The symbol ﷺ is a single Unicode character that writes out a short prayer: _ṣalla Allāhu ʿalayhi wa-sallam_ (صلى الله عليه وسلم). Writers use it after the Prophet's name in place of the full phrase.

## The command in the Qur'an

<Quran
  ref="33:56"
  arabic="إِنَّ اللَّهَ وَمَلَائِكَتَهُ يُصَلُّونَ عَلَى النَّبِيِّ ۚ يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا صَلُّوا عَلَيْهِ وَسَلِّمُوا تَسْلِيمًا"
  translation="Indeed, Allah confers blessing upon the Prophet, and His angels [ask Him to do so]. O you who have believed, ask [Allah to confer] blessing upon him and ask [Allah to grant him] peace."
  translator="Sahih International"
/>

## The reward described by the Prophet

<Hadith
  collection="Sahih Muslim"
  number="408"
  grade="Sahih"
  narrator="Narrated by Abū Hurayra"
  arabic="مَنْ صَلَّى عَلَيَّ وَاحِدَةً صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ عَشْرًا"
>
  Whoever sends blessings upon me once, God sends blessings upon him tenfold.
</Hadith>

Saying the phrase is therefore an act of worship in its own right, not only a mark of respect.[^abbr]

[^abbr]: Older English writing sometimes abbreviates the phrase as “(s)” or “PBUH”. Spelling it out, or using the single character ﷺ, is clearer.
