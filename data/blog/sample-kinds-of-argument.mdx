Every argument on this site is one of three kinds. Knowing which kind you are reading tells you what it can promise, and what it would take to answer it.

## Deduction

A deductive argument claims that if its premises are true, its conclusion _must_ be true. It is **valid** when the conclusion follows, and **sound** when it is valid and its premises are true.

> Whatever begins to exist has a cause. The universe began to exist. So the universe has a cause.

To resist a valid deduction you must deny a premise; you cannot accept them all and refuse the conclusion. Muslim logicians called a deduction from certain premises a <Term translit="burhān">demonstration</Term>.

## Induction

An inductive argument claims that its conclusion is _probable_ given its premises. Every observed case so far supports the general rule; the next case might still break it. Most of what we know about the natural world is known this way.

## Inference to the best explanation

The third kind asks which account best explains the evidence: which is simplest, explains the most, and leaves least unexplained. A detective reasons this way, and so does a historian weighing reports.

## Side by side

| Kind                | Claims                                  | To answer it                       |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Deduction           | The conclusion is certain, given the premises | Deny a premise, or show it invalid |
| Induction           | The conclusion is probable              | Show the sample is unrepresentative |
| Best explanation    | The conclusion explains the evidence best | Offer a better explanation         |

The arguments for God's existence in Part II are mostly deductive. Many of the arguments for the truth of Islam in Part III are arguments to the best explanation: of the Qur'an, of the life of the Prophet ﷺ, of what his followers reported. It helps to know which you are reading.
