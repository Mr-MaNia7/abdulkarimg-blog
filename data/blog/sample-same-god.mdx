## What the Qur'an says

The Qur'an tells Muslims how to speak to Jews and Christians, and it begins from common ground:

<Quran
  ref="29:46"
  arabic="وَإِلَٰهُنَا وَإِلَٰهُكُمْ وَاحِدٌ وَنَحْنُ لَهُ مُسْلِمُونَ"
  translation="…and our God and your God is one; and we are Muslims [in submission] to Him."
  translator="Sahih International"
/>

The verse does not say the two communities believe the same things about God. It says the one they address is the same.<Sidenote>The quotation is the end of a longer verse that begins with an instruction about how to argue with the People of the Book.</Sidenote>

## Where the disagreement lies

The Qur'an's criticism of Christian doctrine is a criticism of description, not of address. Calling God three, or ascribing a son to Him, is what the Qur'an names <Term translit="shirk" />: associating partners with God.

<Callout type="note">
A Christian would answer that the Trinity is not three gods but one God in three persons. A fair answer to this question has to state that position in its own terms before disagreeing with it.
</Callout>

## So, the same God?

In intention, yes: there is no second Creator to worship. In description, Muslims hold that Christian doctrine says things of God that are not true of Him. Both statements are needed for an honest answer.
